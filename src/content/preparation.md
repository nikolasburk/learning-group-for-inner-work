## Change the place

If you've been at your desk all day, don't take the session there too. Move to the couch, an armchair, the floor — anywhere your body doesn't already associate with work. A blanket and a cup of tea do more than they sound like they do.

## It's not a business meeting

It matters that others can see you; it doesn't matter what you look like. Comfortable clothes, no lighting setup, hair however it is. If you'd be more relaxed half-lying under a blanket, be half-lying under a blanket.

## Outside works too

Some people take the session on their phone, from a bench or a quiet spot on a walk. That works well. A café doesn't — it's not private, and you'll be filtering yourself the whole time without noticing.

## Somewhere you won't be overheard

Being overheard changes what you're willing to say, and what's shared in a session stays in the session. Close the door, tell whoever's around that you're unavailable for two hours, and use headphones — as much for the privacy of the others in the room as for your own.

## Leave a buffer

Try not to drop in straight from your last meeting, and try not to schedule anything right after. Close the work tabs, put your phone on do not disturb, and give yourself five quiet minutes on either side. It noticeably changes how you land, and what you take with you afterwards.

## Nothing to prepare

You don't need a topic, an insight, or an update. Whatever state you show up in — tired, scattered, perfectly fine — is the material we work with. Coming in without a plan is a feature.
